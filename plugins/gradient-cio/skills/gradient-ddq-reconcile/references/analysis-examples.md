# Analysis examples

Fictional examples marked **(TEST)** show the required depth. Use the pattern, never these values.

## Discrepancies and follow-ups

**Strong (TEST)**
> **Observation:** Three of 28 claims are Contradicted, including a $420 million AUM gap and an administrator mismatch [S3]. **Why it matters:** The differences affect scale and fund-operations diligence. **Uncertainty:** The DDQ is six months newer than the ADV filing. **What would change the view:** A current filing and administrator confirmation resolving all three rows would remove the contradictions.

**Weak contrast:** “The reconciliation found discrepancies [S3].” It gives no count, subjects, diligence impact, date limitation, or named resolution evidence.

## Firm reconciliation

**Strong (TEST)**
> **Observation:** The DDQ states 64 employees, while the latest Form ADV reports 51, a difference of 13 [S5]. **Why it matters:** The gap may reflect timing or scope and affects operating-scale assessment. **Uncertainty:** The documents use different as-of dates and staffing bases. **What would change the view:** A dated headcount schedule reconciling employees, contractors, and affiliates would resolve the row.

**Weak contrast:** “Headcount does not match [S5].” It omits values, diligence relevance, basis limitation, and the specific reconciliation schedule needed.

## Fund reconciliation — Meridian Credit Fund (TEST)

**Strong (TEST)**
> **Observation:** The DDQ names Apex as administrator, while Schedule D names Beacon for the same fund and period [S7]. **Why it matters:** The mismatch prevents a reliable service-provider conclusion. **Uncertainty:** Schedule D may lag a recent provider change. **What would change the view:** A current administrator agreement or amended filing naming Apex would reconcile the records.
