# Analysis examples

Fictional examples marked **(TEST)** show the required depth. Use the pattern, never these values.

## Target Portfolio Structure

**Strong (TEST)**
> **Observation:** Buyout represents 62% of private-markets NAV versus a 45% target, while secondaries are 4% against a 12% target [S4]. **Why it matters:** The proposed mix reduces strategy concentration and adds a pacing tool. **Uncertainty:** Two fund valuations are one quarter stale. **What would change the view:** A refreshed NAV report showing buyout below 50% would narrow the restructuring need.

**Weak contrast:** “The target structure improves diversification [S4].” It lacks the concentration values, portfolio implication, valuation limitation, and measurable refresh trigger.

## Forward Return and Risk

**Strong (TEST)**
> **Observation:** The target models at 8.1% return and 14.3% volatility, versus 7.6% and 13.7% currently [S7]. **Why it matters:** The modest return increase does not materially change modeled risk efficiency. **Uncertainty:** Private-market assumptions use smoothed quarterly observations. **What would change the view:** A new CMA result with 50 basis points less target volatility would strengthen the case.

**Weak contrast:** “The target has attractive forward characteristics [S7].” It does not quantify the comparison, explain its relevance, name model basis, or define a rerun threshold.

## Liquidity and Denominator Risk

**Strong (TEST)**
> **Observation:** Unfunded commitments equal 13% of NAV, while liquid assets cover 1.6 times twelve months of modeled calls [S9]. **Why it matters:** Coverage leaves limited room for a denominator shock. **Uncertainty:** Call timing depends on manager pacing assumptions. **What would change the view:** Coverage below 1.25 times in the next pacing report would elevate the constraint.

**Weak contrast:** “Liquidity should be monitored [S9].” It supplies no coverage measure, portfolio consequence, pacing limitation, or named threshold.

## Company Look-Through and Diversification

**Strong (TEST)**
> **Observation:** Server canonical-NAV aggregates show Company A at 24% and technology at 41%, with 18/20 companies mapped [S10]. **Why it matters:** This non-additive overlay identifies concentration without increasing commitment exposure. **Uncertainty:** Source coverage is incomplete because two holdings are unmapped; the artifact is **Draft — July 22, 2026**. **What would change the view:** Complete coverage with technology below 35%.

**Weak contrast:** “Look-through adds 24% exposure and confirms diversification [S10].” It double counts company evidence, treats incomplete coverage as complete, conflates an exact source value with a projected range, and omits server basis and draft lineage.
