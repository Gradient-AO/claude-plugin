# Analysis examples

Fictional examples marked **(TEST)** show the required depth. Use the pattern, never these values.

## Firm profile & ownership

**Strong (TEST)**
> (TEST) Form ADV reports $4.8 billion of regulatory AUM, up 19% since the prior filing, while three principals control 82% of voting interests [S2]. The growth increases operating scale, but ownership continuity remains material because the filing gives no current succession plan and the latest amendment is nine months old.

**Weak contrast:** “The firm is growing and ownership is concentrated [S2].” It lacks precise evidence, diligence significance, and the filing limitation.

## Regulatory, conflicts & custody

**Strong (TEST)**
> (TEST) Item 11 discloses one regulatory event dated 2024, and 71% of private-fund assets use an affiliated broker while custody is reported with an independent bank [S4]. The combination warrants conflict review, although Form ADV describes arrangements rather than control effectiveness and no current testing report was available.

**Weak contrast:** “There are some regulatory and conflict issues [S4].” It does not name the event, exposure, custody evidence, or evidence boundary.

## Peer positioning

**Strong (TEST)**
> (TEST) The adviser’s regulatory AUM is at the 64th percentile of its Gradient cohort, but its 11 private funds exceed the cohort median of six [S6]. This describes operating complexity rather than quality; cohort membership is model-defined, and a refreshed peer report could move both percentile and median.

**Weak contrast:** “The manager is larger than peers [S6].” It offers no values and improperly reduces two cohort measures to a quality-like conclusion.

## Fund operations & service providers

**Strong (TEST)**
> (TEST) Two of four reported funds use the same administrator, while one fund’s auditor is absent from the latest Schedule D record [S8]. Shared administration may simplify oversight, but the missing auditor prevents a complete provider assessment because absence in the filing is not proof that no auditor exists.

**Weak contrast:** “Service providers appear mostly consistent [S8].” It obscures the fund count, missing auditor, and limits of Schedule D evidence.

## Reported equity holdings (Form 13F)

**Strong (TEST)**
> (TEST) The latest Form 13F lists 37 positions, with the top five representing 46% of reported value and the largest issuer at 14% [S10]. Concentration is notable within disclosed long-only US-listed equities, but the filing is lagged and excludes shorts, cash, derivatives, private assets, and non-US securities.

**Weak contrast:** “The portfolio is concentrated [S10].” It treats an incomplete firm-level filing as a complete portfolio and gives no supporting values.

## Monitoring, findings & DDQ

**Strong (TEST)**
> (TEST) The roster contains two open High findings, including a 143-day-old administrator confirmation, and the last completed review is 16 months old [S12]. Those dated items make evidence freshness the central monitoring issue, although subject resolution failed for one fund and its independent review history remains unavailable.

**Weak contrast:** “Several findings need monitoring [S12].” It omits severity, age, review timing, and the unresolved-fund coverage gap.
