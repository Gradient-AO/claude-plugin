# Writing Standards — Marketable Alternatives Construction

## Decision language

- Lead with `We recommend <action>, subject to <conditions>` and a separate `Committee action requested`.
- Express allocation changes only when the source supports them.
- Name the mandate and benchmark in the recommendation.
- Never say a committee decision has been made before approval.

## Evidence discipline

- Every number carries `[S#]`; scaling and rounding are allowed, unsupported derivation is not.
- Preserve returned status, basis, units, period, currency, coverage and missing reason.
- Write `Not available — <reason>` for absent evidence.
- Separate historical observation, governed policy assessment and forward assumption.

## Hedge-fund commentary

- Treat crowding and 13F evidence as limited market or long-only equity context, not a complete manager book.
- Do not infer shorts, derivatives, leverage, liquidity, alpha, beta or strategy from a name or label.
- Report redemption terms, gates and side pockets only when directly sourced.
- Do not apply private-markets commitments, pacing, cash-flow or PME analysis to hedge funds.
- Diligence evidence supports conditions and follow-up questions, not claims of manager quality or skill.
- Forward values are assumptions, not forecasts or guarantees.

## Recommendation test

For each proposed action include:

1. sourced observation;
2. mandate or benchmark implication;
3. uncertainty or missing manager terms;
4. proposed action, size and phase;
5. approval condition and review trigger.

Avoid `guaranteed`, `safe`, `uncorrelated`, `all-weather`, `alpha-generating`, `best-in-class` and unsupported
superlatives. Do not use `simulated attribution`, `projected attribution`, legacy local-calculation tags or uncited local
calculations.
