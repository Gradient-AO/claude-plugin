# Analysis examples

Fictional examples marked **(TEST)** show the required depth. Use the pattern, never these values.

## Fundamentals trend

**Strong (TEST)**
> (TEST) Revenue grew 8.4% year over year while operating margin fell from 18.2% to 15.6% across the latest four filed quarters [S2]. Growth therefore came with weaker reported profitability, but the series contains one revised quarter and does not establish whether cost pressure is temporary.

**Weak contrast:** “Revenue increased but margins declined [S2].” It lacks magnitude, period, analytical implication, and the revision limitation.

## Filing changes

**Strong (TEST)**
> (TEST) The latest 10-K added two cybersecurity risk passages and removed the prior statement that supplier concentration was immaterial [S4]. Those textual changes widen the disclosed risk perimeter, although filing-language changes do not prove an event occurred and the comparison covers only the two most recent annual filings.

**Weak contrast:** “The filing contains new risks [S4].” It neither identifies the changes nor explains their significance and comparison limit.

## Risk factors

**Strong (TEST)**
> (TEST) Customer concentration rose to 34% of revenue from 27%, and the latest filing names one contract renewal within twelve months [S5]. The paired disclosures increase dependence on a near-term commercial outcome, but the filing does not provide customer profitability or renewal probability.

**Weak contrast:** “Customer concentration is a concern [S5].” It gives no evidence, timing, decision relevance, or missing-information boundary.

## Latest earnings release

**Strong (TEST)**
> (TEST) Quarterly revenue of $1.26 billion exceeded the prior-year quarter by 6%, while reported operating income declined 11% and guidance was maintained [S7]. The release shows resilient sales with weaker conversion, but management’s non-GAAP reconciliation excludes restructuring costs and no independent consensus estimate is available.

**Weak contrast:** “Earnings were mixed [S7].” It omits the reported values, the sales-margin tension, and the non-GAAP evidence limitation.

## Peers and dated valuation context

**Strong (TEST)**
> (TEST) The issuer’s filed enterprise-value-to-EBITDA measure is 13.4 times versus a 9.8-times median across four confirmed peers as of 30 June [S9]. The difference is dated context rather than a valuation judgment, because peer fiscal periods vary and one company reports under a different segment mix.

**Weak contrast:** “The stock is expensive versus peers [S9].” It uses prohibited valuation language and omits date, peer count, and comparability limits.

## Industry structure

**Strong (TEST)**
> (TEST) The top three disclosed competitors account for 58% of referenced industry revenue, while the issuer reports a 12% share in its principal segment [S11]. Scale may influence bargaining power, but the industry source covers North America only and the issuer’s segment definition is not identical.

**Weak contrast:** “The industry is concentrated [S11].” It lacks market-share evidence, issuer relevance, geography scope, and definition caveat.

## Positioning and crowding

**Strong (TEST)**
> (TEST) Twenty-three reporting hedge-fund advisers disclosed the issuer, placing holder count at the 81st percentile, while aggregate reported value fell 9% quarter over quarter [S13]. The signals indicate broad but easing disclosed ownership, not intent, because Form 13F is delayed and excludes shorts and derivatives.

**Weak contrast:** “The name is crowded [S13].” It converts limited filing evidence into intent and omits the conflicting holder and value signals.
